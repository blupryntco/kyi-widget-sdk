Overview

The KYI (Know Your Issuer) widget lets token issuers on your platform verify their assets with Bluprynt, without leaving your product.

TL;DR

Contact your Bluprynt representative to set up a partner integration.

You send Bluprynt

  • Every origin that will embed the widget, including staging and local development (e.g. https://app.example.com, http://localhost:3000).

Bluprynt sends you

  • Partner ID, the issuer in your tokens.
  • SECRET_KEY, for signing tokens on your server.

Then: npm install @bluprynt/kyi-widget-sdk, generate a token for the logged-in user on your backend, and call kyi() on your frontend. Integration guide →

What it does

Inside the widget, a token issuer verifies their asset like this:

  1. KYB. The issuer completes a Know Your Business check.
  2. Wallet verification. The issuer proves control of the wallets behind the asset.
  3. On-chain identity. Bluprynt creates an on-chain identity for the issuer, which is then subject to ongoing compliance checks.

The SDK is a small JavaScript library that opens this flow as a modal or a drawer on top of your page. Issuers can also use it to view the assets and wallets they have already verified.

How identity works

Your issuers never sign up for Bluprynt. They don't create an account, log in, or authenticate a second time. Your platform has already authenticated them, and a signed access token carries that identity into the widget. From the issuer's point of view, verification is simply part of your product.

The access token contains your internal user ID for the issuer. Bluprynt trusts that ID completely: whatever user ID you put in a token is treated as that issuer, along with their KYB results, verified wallets and on-chain identity.

This means managing user IDs is your responsibility. Each issuer must always get the same ID, no two issuers may share one, and your server must only ever generate a token for the user who is currently logged in. See Choosing a user ID.

How the pieces fit together

  1. You tell Bluprynt which origins will embed the widget. Bluprynt allowlists them and gives you a partner ID (the issuer claim) and a SECRET_KEY.
  2. An issuer logs in to your platform as usual.
  3. Your backend generates a short-lived access token for that issuer, signed with your SECRET_KEY.
  4. Your frontend calls kyi() with the token, and the widget opens.
  5. The issuer completes verification inside the widget. Bluprynt links the result to your user ID.

Your SECRET_KEY stays on your server. The browser only ever sees the short-lived token.
